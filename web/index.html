Virtual Work 1. Principle of Virtual Work & Mechanical Advantage

For an ideal static scissor linkage at equilibrium, infinitesimal virtual displacements yield zero net virtual work. The input mechanical work done by the helical extension spring tension \(T\) over the upper rhombus vertical compression \(\delta H\) strictly equals the output work done lifting the suspended payload \(P_{\text{eff}}\) through total mechanism stroke \(\delta L\):

$$\delta A_{\text{in}} = \delta A_{\text{out}} \iff T \cdot \delta H = P_{\text{eff}} \cdot \delta L$$

Defining kinematic transmission ratio \(\lambda = \frac{\delta L}{\delta H}\), the ideal mechanical tension is:

$$T_{\text{ideal}} = \lambda \cdot P_{\text{eff}}$$

Thermodynamics 2. Total Parasitic Energy Dissipation & Hinge Friction

In physical 3D-printed/machined hardware, each scissor stage has multiple rotating pivot pins (top, middle scissor cross, bottom). During extension or retraction through angle change \(\Delta \theta_i\), dry Coulomb contact friction and viscous bearing resistance dissipate mechanical energy into irreversible thermal losses:

$$\Delta E_{\text{dissipated}} = \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{hinges}}} \int M_{\text{fric}, i}(\theta_i) \, d\theta_i$$

Where the frictional joint moment depends on normal pin reaction force \(R_i\), pin radius \(r_{\text{pin}}\), and friction coefficient \(\mu\):

$$M_{\text{fric}, i} = \mu \cdot R_i \cdot r_{\text{pin}}$$

Introducing real-world mechanical transmission efficiency \(\eta \in [0.50, 1.00]\), the actual required input spring tension becomes amplified:

$$A_{\text{out}} = \eta \cdot A_{\text{in}} \iff T = \frac{\lambda \cdot P_{\text{eff}}}{\eta}$$

Kinematics 3. Multi-Linkage Architectures & Transmission Ratios (\(\lambda\))

The total downward mechanism extension \(L(m)\) relates to the upper monitoring rhombus height \(H(m)\) through geometry:

  • Rig A (Uniform \(n=3\)): Three identical scissor rhombi in series.
    $$L_A = 3 \cdot H \implies \lambda_A = \frac{dL_A}{dH} = 3.000\times$$
  • Rig C (Progressive \(1:2:3\) Variable-Pitch Scissor): Links scaled with arithmetic progression \(\{1\ell, 2\ell, 3\ell\}\).
    $$L_C = (1 + 2 + 3) \cdot H_0 = 6 \cdot H_0 \implies \lambda_C = \frac{dL_C}{dH_0} = 6.000\times$$
  • Rig B (Uniform \(n=5\)): Five uniform scissor stages in series.
    $$L_B = 5 \cdot H \implies \lambda_B = \frac{dL_B}{dH} = 5.000\times$$

Equilibrium 4. Effective Gravitational Force & Celestial Acceleration (\(g\))

The effective vertical load \(P_{\text{eff}}\) accounts for suspended payload mass \(m\) plus the distributed structural mass of PLA scissor links \(m_{\text{link}}\) evaluated at their effective center of mass under arbitrary planetary gravity \(g\):

$$P_{\text{eff}} = \left(m + m_{\text{struct}}\right) \cdot g$$
$$m_{\text{struct}} = \begin{cases} \frac{n}{2} \cdot m_{\text{link}}, & \text{Uniform Rigs (A: } 1.5 \, m_{\text{link}}, \text{ B: } 2.5 \, m_{\text{link}}\text{)} \\[6pt] 3.0 \cdot m_{\text{link}}, & \text{Progressive Rig C } (1:2:3) \end{cases}$$

Elasticity 5. Hooke's Law Deflection Equations

Combining linear elasticity \(T = k \cdot \Delta H\) with the mechanical transmission ratio yields the analytical deflection profiles:

$$\Delta H(m) = \frac{T(m)}{k} = \frac{\lambda \cdot P_{\text{eff}}}{\eta \cdot k}$$
$$\Delta L(m) = \lambda \cdot \Delta H(m) = \frac{\lambda^2 \cdot P_{\text{eff}}}{\eta \cdot k} = \frac{\lambda^2 \cdot (m + m_{\text{struct}}) \cdot g}{\eta \cdot k}$$

Regression 6. Stiffness Extraction via Linear Regression Slope

Differentiating \(\Delta H\) with respect to suspended mass \(m\) reveals the experimental slope \(\tan(\alpha)\):

$$\tan(\alpha) = \frac{\partial (\Delta H)}{\partial m} = \frac{\lambda \cdot g}{\eta \cdot k} \iff k = \frac{\lambda \cdot g}{\eta \cdot \tan(\alpha)}$$

From laboratory linear regression tests: \(k \approx 981 \text{ N/m}\) (Rig A, \(R^2 = 0.998\)) and \(k \approx 1090 \text{ N/m}\) (Rig B, \(R^2 = 0.996\)).

Dynamics 7. 2nd-Order Dynamic Harmonic Vibration ODE

The transient response under sudden load step changes or impulse kicks satisfies the damped harmonic oscillator equation:

$$\frac{d^2 H}{dt^2} + 2 \zeta \omega_n \frac{dH}{dt} + \omega_n^2 \left(H(t) - H_{\text{target}}\right) = 0$$

Where the natural circular frequency \(\omega_n\) is dynamically coupled to spring stiffness \(k\) and reflected mechanism inertia:

$$\omega_n = \sqrt{\frac{k_{\text{eff}}}{m_{\text{eff}}}} = \sqrt{\frac{k}{\frac{m + m_{\text{struct}}}{\lambda^2}}}$$

The viscous damping coefficient \(c\) is regulated by the user-selectable damping ratio \(\zeta \in [0.00, 0.60]\):

$$c = 2 \cdot \zeta \cdot \omega_n \cdot m_{\text{eff}}$$
